Patio

Steps

Retaining Wall

Dry stack walls are built up to 4′ high. Any higher requires cinder block, rebar and mortar on a concrete footer.

Process

This is a general description of what to expect from day one to completion. Patios are the most involved so that's what I've described here. Walls and steps follow the same basic procedure.

Excavation starts after surveying the site and marking the perimeter. All roots are removed from the area and six inches of dirt is displaced or taken to a recycling center.

The trench is filled with a gravel foundation that measures four inches deep. This provides a solid base for the patio, prevents weed growth and allows water to seep through which prevents shifting through freeze thaw cycles.

The foundation is leveled and compacted then flagstone is distributed evenly by size and cut so the space between each slab does not exceed one inch. Excess stone gets broken down and used as backfill or discarded at a recycling center.

Next the flagstone is leveled from the highest point, usually by the side of the house. A very slight (unnoticeable) drop in level is used to direct water. Dry stack retaining walls are set with a gentle tilt backwards to compensate for the weight of the backfill.

Gaps are packed with crusher fines (breeze) which harden over time but won’t crack like mortar.

Large Projects

I hire sub contractors if the scope of work calls for it although most projects can be finished single handedly within 4-6 weeks. One example of a two person job is pouring concrete, one person needs to be mixing while the other pours and shapes the form.

Stonework is generally linear. One brick, one slab after the last. I built an 80 foot long by 4 foot tall retaining wall in sixteen days (4 days a week) by myself. It took five palates of stone (20,000 pounds total) plus the weight of several hundred five gallon buckets filled to the brim with gravel and sand.

About - Beginning

Redstone Masonry is not a traditional business. I became self employed in 2021 after learning on the job for a year. My employer sent me a dozen leads and one person gave me a chance. I have him to thank for all of my subsequent clients. To this day every job I've done was from word of mouth.

I don't plan on scaling because the style and quality of the product would change. I take pride in my craft, my work is unique and I intend to keep it that way. I have no interest in doing administrative work. I enjoy baking in the sun and lifting heavy stones.

Meaning and Purpose

Lots of people comment on the physical toll of this trade but I haven't given it a second thought for years. At first it was very difficult. I wasn't used to being in the sun all day, I had allergies, I struggled to breathe through the respirator in 95 degree heat. During peak summer I drank a gallon of water every day and still went home with a headache. In the winter I was cold and wet all day.

I became acclimated to the lifestyle and even started to enjoy it. Eventually my enjoyment became gratitude. I appreciate the cliche that enlightenment is found through self destruction. I don't believe in optimization. I have no use for protein powder, calorie counting, exogenous testosterone, diet fads, training programs, gym memberships or popcorn muscle.


Struggle

I was underweight for my height when I started. I steadily gained weight over four years then lost a significant amount because of an illness that took over a year to recover from.

Unfortunately a lot of medical "professionals" are incompetent, complacent, overworked, undereducated or all of the above. All three doctors I spoke to failed to diagnose a fairly common ailment. The second one, a so-called specialist, wanted to perform invasive tests which likely would've worsened my condition. Western medicine is good for resetting broken bones.

I still worked the entire time which was unpleasant to say the least. After experiencing months of debilitating physical pain and existential dread from living with an unknown illness of unknown cause I gained new perspective on life.

Shortly before discovering the herbal medicines that would cure me I pulled an all time best deadlift of 375 pounds which was done in the dirt from a two inch deficit in the middle of February.


Mindset

I didn't move the weight because my health metrics were optimal. On the contrary I was likely nutrient deficent because my body refused to digest most foods. All I ate for months was corn tortillas and dried beef that I prepared myself.

Rage allowed me to pull over 2.5 times my body weight during one of the most chaotic and uncertain periods of my life.

So called "optimization" is nonsense invented and promoted by ad agencies and fitness grifters to sell toxic sludge and overpriced whore-core "clothing" to social media junkies.

People are not meant to drink soylent and exercise in a fluorescent lit warehouse. Human beings have survived countless droughts and famines. We are built for struggle.

If you doubt yourself for a second you won't move the weight. This mentality can be applied to all areas of life.


Conclusion

I never got a proper diagnoses so I can only guess based on what the cure was. I think stress inhibited sufficient production of stomach acid which caused an overgrowth of bacteria in my digestive system. I took a handful of herbal medicines over the course of six weeks and rotated them to ensure they didn't lose potency.

After a month I started to feel better and gradually tapered off. What seemed to work best was neem and oregano oil, I also took caprylic acid and betaine hcl. During the herbal medicine regimen I mixed powder charcoal with water and drank it four hours after a dose.

Before getting sick I worked the door at a high capacity nightclub. I did seven days a week for months, four days moving rocks and three nights at the club. The illness began with physical depletion then nausea after eating. Eventually pain and nausea woke me up every morning. I could handle the stress mentally but my body gave out.

I encourage anyone reading this to look after themselves. Life is nothing without health. I worked myself sick out of fear of being destitute. We're constantly bled through taxation and inflation so I see no point in sacrificing more life force than necessary.
